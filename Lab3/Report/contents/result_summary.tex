\section{Synthesis and Results Summary}

\subsection{Synthesis target and generated artifacts}

The RTL design of \texttt{Cubic\_Solver} was synthesized using Cadence Genus under the \texttt{slow} operating condition. The synthesized result corresponds to the Lab 3 floating-point cubic-equation solver rather than the SPI design used in earlier report drafts.

The main generated artifacts referenced in this section are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item synthesized netlist: \texttt{Lab3/synthesis/Cubic\_Solver\_m.v}
    \item area report: \texttt{Lab3/synthesis/final\_area.rpt}
    \item QoR report: \texttt{Lab3/synthesis/final\_qor.rpt}
    \item timing report: \texttt{Lab3/synthesis/final\_time.rpt}
\end{itemize}

The generated netlist preserves the intended top-level interface of the solver, including the control signals \texttt{clk}, \texttt{rst\_n}, \texttt{start}, \texttt{done}, the FP32 coefficient inputs \texttt{FP\_a} to \texttt{FP\_d}, and the six FP32 root outputs that represent the real and imaginary parts of three roots.

\subsection{Timing report}

The detailed timing report \texttt{final\_time.rpt} confirms that the worst setup paths are marked \texttt{MET}. The most critical paths are located inside the pipelined divider, especially around the radix-4 stages under \texttt{Div\_FP\_0/gen\_radix\_pipe[\dots]}. This confirms that the divider is not only the largest block in area, but also the timing bottleneck of the whole design.

\begin{itemize}
    \item the current Lab 3 implementation is functionally synthesizable and timing-clean at the selected clock constraint
    \item future optimization effort should focus first on the divider datapath if a faster operating frequency or lower area is required
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conclusion}

The synthesis result for Lab 3 demonstrates that the FP32 cubic-equation solver can be implemented successfully as a gate-level hardware system. The most important conclusions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the synthesized top-level module matches the intended interface of \texttt{Cubic\_Solver}
    \item the total synthesized area is 68354.103 $\mu m^2$ with 17425 cells
    \item the floating-point divider is the dominant submodule in both area and timing
    \item the design meets the 10 ns timing requirement with zero violating paths, although the worst slack is exactly 0 ps
\end{itemize}

Overall, the synthesis reports are consistent with the RTL analysis presented in previous sections: the design is arithmetic-intensive, structurally large compared with simpler control-oriented labs, and primarily limited by the complexity of the floating-point division pipeline.
