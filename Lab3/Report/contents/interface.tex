\section{Interface}

\subsection{Top-level module: \texttt{Cubic\_Solver}}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{graphics/Cubic_Solver_Interface.pdf}
    \caption{Interface of the \texttt{Cubic\_Solver} module.}
    \label{fig:cubic_solver_interface}
\end{figure}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Signal description of \texttt{Cubic\_Solver}}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|p{8.1cm}|}
\hline
\textbf{Signal} & \textbf{In/Out} & \textbf{Width} & \textbf{Description} \\ \hline
\texttt{clk} & Input & 1 & System clock used to drive the controller and all floating-point operators. \\ \hline
\texttt{rst\_n} & Input & 1 & Active-low reset signal. Clears all internal registers and returns the FSM to \texttt{IDLE}. \\ \hline
\texttt{start} & Input & 1 & Handshake signal that starts one solving transaction for the current coefficient set. \\ \hline
\texttt{done} & Output & 1 & Completion flag. Goes high when all three roots have been generated and written to output registers. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_a} & Input & 32 & FP32 coefficient $a$ of the cubic equation. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_b} & Input & 32 & FP32 coefficient $b$ of the cubic equation. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_c} & Input & 32 & FP32 coefficient $c$ of the cubic equation. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_d} & Input & 32 & FP32 coefficient $d$ of the cubic equation. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_x0\_re} & Output & 32 & Real part of the first root. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_x0\_im} & Output & 32 & Imaginary part of the first root. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_x1\_re} & Output & 32 & Real part of the second root. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_x1\_im} & Output & 32 & Imaginary part of the second root. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_x2\_re} & Output & 32 & Real part of the third root. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_x2\_im} & Output & 32 & Imaginary part of the third root. \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The top-level solver receives one set of cubic coefficients in IEEE-754 single precision and returns three roots in complex form. Each root is represented by a pair of independent FP32 outputs so that both real-root and complex-root cases can be handled by the same hardware interface.

\subsection{Floating-point adder: \texttt{Add\_FP}}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{graphics/Add_Interface.pdf}
    \caption{Interface of the \texttt{Add\_FP} module.}
    \label{fig:add_interface}
\end{figure}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Signal description of \texttt{Add\_FP}}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|p{8.3cm}|}
\hline
\textbf{Signal} & \textbf{In/Out} & \textbf{Width} & \textbf{Description} \\ \hline
\texttt{clk} & Input & 1 & Pipeline clock of the floating-point adder. \\ \hline
\texttt{in1} & Input & 32 & First FP32 operand. \\ \hline
\texttt{in2} & Input & 32 & Second FP32 operand. \\ \hline
\texttt{data\_out} & Output & 32 & FP32 addition/subtraction result after alignment, normalization and rounding. \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

This module is used not only for direct additions but also for subtractive operations by changing the sign bit of one operand. In the cubic solver datapath, it is repeatedly reused to accumulate algebraic terms, create scaled versions such as $3S$ or $2P^3$, and reconstruct the final roots.

\subsection{Floating-point multiplier: \texttt{Mul\_FP}}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{graphics/Mul_interface.pdf}
    \caption{Interface of the \texttt{Mul\_FP} module.}
    \label{fig:mul_interface}
\end{figure}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Signal description of \texttt{Mul\_FP}}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|p{8.3cm}|}
\hline
\textbf{Signal} & \textbf{In/Out} & \textbf{Width} & \textbf{Description} \\ \hline
\texttt{clk} & Input & 1 & Pipeline clock of the floating-point multiplier. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_in1} & Input & 32 & First FP32 operand. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_in2} & Input & 32 & Second FP32 operand. \\ \hline
\texttt{FP\_out} & Output & 32 & FP32 multiplication result after mantissa multiplication, normalization and rounding. \\ \hline

The multiplier is the main arithmetic engine for producing powers such as $b^2$, $b^3$, $\Delta_1^2$, $\Delta_0^3$, $P^2$, $P^3$, and for evaluating the complex products required in the cube-root branch.
